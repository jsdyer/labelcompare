Where the data comes from

Open Food Facts is a community-maintained database. Barcode lookups use its public v3 API. Country listings describe where a product has been reported, not where your particular package was made or sold. Saved labels show their retrieval date and each record’s last edit date. Expand “More label details & photos” for categories, reported claims, origins, manufacturing places, stores, packaging and original label photos when supplied. Blank fields mean not reported; the listed market does not establish manufacturing origin.

Comparing portions and missing information

The 100 g / 100 mL view uses source-normalized nutrient fields. Millilitres and grams are kept distinct. When the record does not establish its unit, we label it unknown. The serving view only shows published serving values when a serving description exists; it does not invent a portion or convert densities. Sodium, cholesterol and minerals are converted from the source’s standardized grams to milligrams. Vitamins A, D and B12 use micrograms. Additional nutrient rows appear when at least one selected record reports them. No prepared-food conversion is applied.

Your data and downloads

Your comparison stays in this tab unless you download a receipt. Barcodes you explicitly look up, your IP and normal connection information go to Open Food Facts. Product images load from its image service. Imported receipts are read locally. No account or dietary profile is stored. The optional receipt-history ledger below is stored on this device only when you explicitly select Save history.

JSON receipts preserve the selected source records and retrieval times so you can reopen them. CSV includes the selected nutrient basis, units, sources and dates. A receipt is a dated copy, not a guarantee that the label is current.
