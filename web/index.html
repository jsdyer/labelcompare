This is a comparison of published food records, with a link back to every label. The saved examples are starting points; you can look up any supported barcode.

Where the data comes from

Open Food Facts is a community-maintained database. Barcode lookups use its public v3 API. Country listings describe where a product has been reported, not where your particular package was made or sold. Saved labels show their retrieval date and each record’s last edit date.

Open Food Facts · API documentation

Comparing portions and missing information

The 100 g / 100 mL view uses source-normalized nutrient fields. Millilitres and grams are kept distinct. When the record does not establish its unit, we label it unknown. The serving view only shows published serving values when a serving description exists; it does not invent a portion or convert densities. Sodium is converted from the source’s grams to milligrams. No prepared-food conversion is applied.

Your data and downloads

Your comparison stays in this tab unless you download a receipt. Barcodes you explicitly look up, your IP and normal connection information go to Open Food Facts. Product images load from its image service. Imported receipts are read locally. No account, shopping history or dietary profile is stored here.

JSON receipts preserve the selected source records and retrieval times so you can reopen them. CSV includes the selected nutrient basis, units, sources and dates. A receipt is a dated copy, not a guarantee that the label is current.

Reuse and expanding the tool
